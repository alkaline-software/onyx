# Design

The standard every part of Onyx a person sees is held to: the window, the sidebar, the note, the settings, the icon. The bar is the work that wins Awwwards, the Webbys and the FWA: minimal, calm, intuitive, finished in every state. The studio's constitution is where this comes from; this file is Onyx's own, in its terms, and is its standard. How the window's parts work is in `docs/window.md`, the editor in `docs/editor.md`.

## Rules

- **Calm.** One accent, the person's choice of seven, for what is on, in use, chosen, or the one thing a page is for: a link, inline code's plate, a quote's bar, a done task's box, the chosen tile. Nothing else competes with it.
- **Everything fits.** Every section of the settings shows all it has without scrolling. The page still scrolls for a window made smaller.
- **The canvas has one rhythm.** Every height, gap and pad of a note comes from `Rhythm` (`layout.rs`) and `onyx_core::rhythm`, in whole pixels, scaled from the words' size: a body line of 1.6, code of 1.5, and the room over a heading a share of the body's line. Nothing on the canvas sets a height of its own (`docs/editor.md`).
- **Nothing jumps.** What appears, goes, opens, closes, grows, moves or turns does so over a moment, with the pattern its neighbors use (`docs/window.md`, Motion). No pause at the end of a motion.
- **What is cut says so.** A list or a note that runs past an edge fades out at that edge, and the fade grows and goes with the scroll, so nothing snaps on at the first pixel or off at the last. What is under a fade counts as out of sight: it takes no press, and a caret is never there.
- **A gesture stays where it began.** A scroll moves only the scroller it started in (`scroll.rs`).
- **Words are few.** A row's name says what it is. The window's chrome says nothing the note does not need. No sentence explaining a concept, and no hint in an empty list.
- **No square corner inside a round one.** A clip in gpui is a rectangle, `overflow_hidden` too: anything that paints a ground (a chosen row, a hover, a band, a picture) where it meets a rounded card's corner stands square past the curve. It rounds its own corners there, by the card's radius less its border. Before saying a rounded thing is done, look at each of its corners with what can stand in them lit.
- **One way to draw each thing.** A setting is a row on a card; on or off is a switch; a choice among a few is a segmented control; a button is a pill with an icon before its word; a face is a tile drawn in itself; a key is a cap. A new control is a new pattern for every page, or it is not made.
- **Both appearances, every state.** Light and dark are designed together. A change is looked at in both, and in every state it has: no vault, a vault with no note, a note, each mode, a menu, the rename sheet, a narrow window.
- **Readable.** Words read on their ground at 4.5 to 1 or better. A key is drawn as a key.
- **Look before saying it is done** (`docs/probing.md`).

## The palette

The neutrals are the studio's, in `themes/onyx.json`: the page `#FBFBFA` and `#1A1918`, the ink `#1F1E1D` and `#F3F1EA`, the muted `#77746B` and `#A09D94`, the panel `#F0EFED` and `#222120`, the border `#DFDFDC` and `#32312E`, the frame a warm graphite `#3D3A36` and `#3A3733`. The frame is the sidebar's ground in both appearances, so the note's page is a well in a lighter shell. Code's inks are the studio's Rosé Pine set (`look::Inks`); they color the reading view's code blocks.

### The accent

Onyx offers seven accents (`look::ACCENTS`, chosen in Settings, General), each a stone, and wears **teal**, the first, on a new install: `#277580` in the light appearance, `#6FB9C4` in the dark. Onyx is the black stone, and the studio's navy is the black that is not black; teal is the farthest of them from Obsidian's purple, and on the navy it reads as the sheen on polished stone. The others go round the wheel from teal, cool to warm and back, so the row of swatches reads as one band: sapphire, amethyst, rose (quartz), carnelian, amber and jade. They are Onyx's own, not Emaki's: each at one lightness and a chroma near teal's, which is the most the gamut gives teal's hue, so none is louder than the default. An accent from before the stones (periwinkle, orchid, moss) is read as the nearest stone (`look::accent`). The icon's band of light is teal's dark shade whatever accent is worn.

## The type

- **The chrome** is the system's face: the sidebar, the strip, the settings, the sheets.
- **A note's words** are in three faces of the person's choice, as emaki sets a conversation (Settings, Font, `fonts.rs`): its **English font**, which is every script but Chinese, Japanese and Korean, its **CJK font**, and its **monospace font** for code. The two text faces never share a character: each run of text is cut where its writing changes and each stretch set in its own face (`fonts::split_cjk`, `fonts::is_cjk`: Han, kana, Hangul, Bopomofo and the punctuation and full-width forms made for them; quotes and dashes the scripts share stay English). So an English face with Chinese in it (Songti SC, a custom face) sets none of that Chinese, and a face wanted for both is chosen for both. The English faces are the eight emaki offers under the same keys: four serifs (Source Serif 4, Newsreader, Literata, Lora) and four sans serifs (Inter to begin with, IBM Plex Sans, Source Sans 3, Geist), each carried as a static file a weight under the SIL Open Font License. The CJK faces (`fonts::CJK`): Auto, the default, which is Noto Serif SC beside a serif English face and Noto Sans SC beside a sans serif; Noto Serif SC and Noto Sans SC, the simplified Chinese subsets of the Source Han faces, regular and bold; LXGW WenKai, a kai hand, regular and medium. Behind the CJK face stand the system's faces of the English face's kind for Japanese, Korean and the rest (Songti SC or PingFang SC, Hiragino, Apple SD Gothic Neo), and the system's emoji last (`fonts::cjk_behind`). The six CJK files are most of the bundle's fonts, about 90 MB in all, which the person chose over the system's faces so a note looks the same on every Mac. Each row's tiles are drawn in their own face: the English ones' "Ag", the CJK ones' names in their own writing over the English (思源宋体 over Noto Serif SC, 思源黑体, 霞鹜文楷; Auto's 汉字 in the face it gets), code's `fn(0) -> 1`. Each row ends in a **Custom** tile, the English one a fifth column as tall as both its rows: it shows the face of the system's chosen for it, drawn in it and named in its own writing and in English where it has both, or Custom while there is none. A press chooses its face where it has one and is not in use, else opens the list of the system's faces under or over the tile, where there is more room: a search field over the families (by either name, word by word, ↑, ↓ and ↩ in the field), the face chosen ticked and in view; the CJK list holds the families with 汉, あ or 한, the monospace list the monospaced ones, read once off the main thread by Core Text (`fonts::system_families`). Only the rows in view are drawn, each in the window's face. A × at the tile's corner, coming and going over a moment, puts its face away, and where it was in use the row's first face takes its place. Choosing another tile keeps the custom face for later (`EditorConfig::text_custom`, `cjk_custom`, `mono_custom`, used under the key `custom`; a file from when a typed name stood over the tiles is read so, `EditorConfig::key_custom`). Unlike emaki's, a family's styles do not open in the list: a family is chosen whole. The note's name over its words is set as its words are: the toolkit's field cuts its runs through the same split (`InputBaseState::set_split_runs`, UPSTREAM.md, `fonts::split_for_fields`).
- **Code** is in a monospace face of the person's choice: JetBrains Mono to begin with, Monaco from the system on a Mac (IBM Plex Mono, carried, elsewhere), Maple Mono or Geist Mono. Its size is its own (`TextSizes::code`), not a share of the words', set beside theirs on one row in Settings.
- **The name** Onyx, in the sidebar's wordmark and About, is Optima where the system has it, else Belleza, carried. Whether the system has it is asked once (`fonts::wordmark_family`): listing every installed face costs about a tenth of a second on a Mac with many fonts, and asked at every frame it held every keystroke and click that long.
- **Sizes.** The words are 14, 16 or 18 points (Small, Medium, Large), on a line of 1.6. Headings go by Craft's scale of the words' size (its title, subtitle, heading and strong text, 24, 22, 20 and 18 over 17, from the page style it ships): 1.412, 1.294, 1.176, 1.059, 1 and 1, the first bold and the rest semibold, on lines of 1.25 to 1.5, with a third of an em over a heading that does not follow a blank line, so a heading leads its section without shouting over it. Code is 0.9 of the words' size on a line of 1.5. The note's name over its words is 1.5 times bold, a step over the top heading. Every format of Export takes these same numbers (`rhythm::HEADINGS`, `rhythm::TITLE`). Tried and dropped: Obsidian's golden-ratio scale (1.618 down), whose second level stood louder than Craft's title.
- The chrome's sizes are the studio's: a section's name 15 semibold, a row's name 13, a control's text 12.5, a group's name 12 semibold in the muted ink, a row's line 11.5 on 15.

## Icons in the window

Tabler's outline icons at a stroke of 1.75, under the names the toolkit asks for, in `assets/icons/`; the tree's folders wear Catppuccin's icons (`file_icons.rs`), Latte in the light appearance and Mocha in the dark, and a note wears none: every note is markdown, so the icon said nothing, and its name stands where a folder's icon would (an empty slot left a gap before every note). A folder may wear one of Tabler's whole outline set instead (3.31.0, every icon, at Tabler's own stroke of 2) in a color of its own (`folder_look.rs`): in the tree, on its pill of tabs, in both windows. Its colors are ten of the app's, each holding 3 to 1 on the window's ground in both appearances, and the person's own, made on a square of saturation and brightness over a bar of hue or by hex; the default icon takes a color as any other does: Catppuccin's folder is one line, drawn as a mask in the folder's color (`folder_look::drawn`), so the grid's first square is the folder as the tree draws it, and Tabler's own folders are not offered beside it. The strip's modes wear a pencil, a file of code and an open book. Every button has an icon before its word; a choice in a menu has one too. A key is drawn on a cap (`widgets::key_text`).

## The app icon

A note on a stone: a plump pebble of onyx, level and the same on both sides, lit from within (Moonlit): its deepest navy, a lighter navy rising to the far shoulder, teal's dark shade caught in its lower edge and glowing on the ground under it in place of a shadow, and light along its rim from the top round the shoulder, bright at the edge and falling off inward (Edge light); and a square note set askew on it, so the stone shows at the note's edges; three lines of writing, the amber one in the middle, and a pen on the note in the navy with its nib at the amber line's end, running up to the top right, where the studio's violet spark was until v0.5.0 (the vault page's pen, writing the same line), on the studio's cream washed with a fifth of teal (`#e1e8e2` to `#c9d2c5`: the cream gradient's ends mixed with the mean of teal's two base shades, `#277580` and `#6FB9C4`, 16% at the top left and 24% at the bottom right), so the Dock tells Onyx from the studio's other icons by its ground while the drawing stays in the studio's hand. The constitution names the mix; five strengths were tried, and a fifth was the one where the ground read as the app's own without losing the cream. The note is the note-taker's thing; the stone it rests on makes it Onyx, and read as one thing where a stone beside the note read as an add-on.

`scripts/icon/icon.html` draws it, with fifteen candidates of the note on the stone under `CANDIDATES`, each drawn on its own pixels at every size and proofed at 32 and 16; `VARIANT` names the one in use and `scripts/make-icon.sh` renders every size into `assets/icon/`. Below 32 pixels the three lines become two thicker ones, the band grows a little, and the pen loses its slit and is drawn thicker. The sixteenth candidate, Writing on a moonlit pebble, is the one in use since v0.5.1: Writing's note and pen on the stone the menu bar's item and the vault page share, lit as the vault page's (`moonlit`). The teal was always the stone's light; drawn as a straight band across it, it became a groove in the item's one ink, and the stone read as a coffee bean. The stone is now a superellipse with a flatter top and a fuller belly (`mP`, `mStone`), lit from the upper right, since the note covers its upper left. Tried and dropped on the way: a teal crescent of shine alone, which read as another color and not as shine; a hard white dot of glint, which sat on the stone instead of in its light; and eight other finishes (a cabochon's bloom, bands, a teal rim, a wet stone's window, satin, strata, two glints, iridescence). Before it the eleventh, Writing, was in use. The first is the same stone and note with the spark in the pen's place, as the icon was before; the next nine vary the stone's shape and tilt, the note's size and corners, and where the two sit, and each lost to it for showing less of the stone's band or less of the note. The last five are the pen in the spark's place: writing on the note, leaning at its corner, laid across its foot, lying on the stone in the paper's white, and standing by it in the amber; writing won for being what the vault page shows, and the pen that lies on the stone had to leave the navy to be seen at all. Tried and dropped before them: the stone beside the note (an add-on), a stone of straight edges (the five-sided one hard to look at), and a faceted gem (too many angles).

## The README's picture

The README opens with one moving picture (`.github/readme.gif`): what Onyx does, played as a person does it, drawn in the hand of the studio's sites and never recorded from the screen. A recording carries one machine's fonts and whatever was open, and goes stale with every change of the window; a drawing is made again in seconds. It stands at the head of the README (`<p align="center"><img src=".github/readme.gif" alt="…" width="100%"></p>`, the alt saying what plays). Onyx's story, under the title Notes that read as you write: Plans is picked in the sidebar and opens in a tab beside Ideas, the pill's plate sliding to it; `## This week` and `Ship the **picture**` are typed into it, their marks muted while the caret is on the line and hidden once it leaves, the heading and the bold word drawn as they read; a task's box is ticked and its words struck; the source opens beside the note from the strip's left button, the file as written in code's face, and closes again. At the right, the four moves, lit in teal.

- **The hand.** Hairlines on warm paper (`#F3F1EA`), a window a sheet of `#F8F6F0` with an edge of `#8E8A80`, words in `#1F1E1D` and `#77746B`; each part a pale wash of its color (9%) with an edge of it, its name in its color; lines stand for words. Source Serif 4 for the name, the title and what a part is called, its italic for what something does, JetBrains Mono for a key or a short label, all from Google Fonts so every machine renders the same picture.
- **The layout.** In a 1920 by 1008 box: the icon and the name (with its kanji, where it has one) at the top left, the system at the top right in the accent, and the title under them, one line of what Onyx is for. The stage at the left; at the right a list of what plays, a row each with its keys on caps, lit in its color while it plays and a shade darker once it has.
- **The story** is what Onyx has now. Each move is what a person does (a click on what does it, a drag, a key), with the pointer the Mac draws, on the curves the window uses for it. For the loop the stage fades out where it ended and in as it began, so the story never runs backward.

`scripts/readme/readme.html` is the drawing: a canvas page that plays in a browser and draws any moment on its own. It keeps a script of times and works every place out from it, and exposes `ready`, `FRAMES` and `frame(i)`, which returns frame i as a PNG; nothing else in the page is named `frame`, or the two call each other. `scripts/make-readme-gif.sh` renders it on a Mac with ffmpeg: `scripts/readme/render.swift` loads the page in a windowless WebKit view and writes every frame, the icon handed in as data (`README_ICON`: drawn from a file it taints the canvas, and no frame can be read back), and ffmpeg makes the GIF with one palette for the whole loop, at 30 frames a second, 12 to 15 seconds and about 3 MB at most. `scripts/make-readme-gif.sh <dir> <first> <last>` writes frames as PNGs to look at first: a sheet of every 45th frame, each move's middle, and the loop's last and first frames side by side. Two things fading in one place stand over each other in a smudge, so one goes before the other comes.

A release that changes what the picture shows (a part, its place, its keys, how it is brought in) changes the drawing in the same pass, and the GIF is rendered again and committed with it.

## The window

Laid out as the studio's windows are, after emaki's: the frame is the window's ground, the sidebar stands on it with no edge of its own, and the note is a card with rounded corners (12 on a Mac) on a band of the frame 4 wide. The sidebar's button and the search's stand beside the traffic lights; folded, the sidebar is a rail at the window's left with the traffic lights stacked at its head and the two buttons under them, a size larger. The window opens at 1080 by 720 and is never narrower than 720.

- **The sidebar**, 268 wide: the app's icon and the wordmark at the right of the title row, the vault's card (the tabs of Notes and Assets at its top; under Notes the vault's own row, as the menu bar note's, with Collapse All Folders, New Note and New Folder always at its right, and the tree, each folder's row with its own New Note and New Folder under the pointer), the vault's button under it, which names the vault shown and lists the container's vaults, New Vault… and Close Vault, as wide as itself (before a vault is shown, the card lists the vaults, each with its mark, and there is no button), and at the foot the person's picture, their name and the settings' gear. A folder's row has a chevron and folds in motion; a note's row wears the Markdown icon; the open note's row stands on a plate. A right click on a row opens its menu. Folded, it is a rail: the New Note disc and the notes card's button, which flies the card out over the page, with the app's icon and the person's picture at its foot.

- **The tabs' row** has a button at each end, as Emaki's panel buttons are, its icon alone: the source at its left, the terminal at its right, gray at rest and in the ink while on, with a short line of the accent under it that grows from its middle, each as far from the card's side as from its top. The source pane is a sheet of its own at the page's left, the terminal one on its own ground at the right, the note's page between.
- **The path bar** under the strip, on the page's tint with no line above or below it: the note's name with its folder before it, in code's face. ⌘F slides a find bar in under it.
- **The note** is a sheet on the page: a column of the card's ground with a hairline round it on a tint of the muted shade, so the words' room and the margins read apart, at its own width while it is alone on the page and full width while a pane stands beside it, the same room for the words at both, with its name over its words in the words' face and a soft line under the name. The name can be typed in, which renames the file, with no border or ground of its own until it has the caret. Its scroller fades at the top and the foot while there is more beyond, and at the note's end the sheet's rounded foot shows whole.
- **The search**, ⌘K: a palette over the window with the notes the phrase is in, each with its first lines and the phrase marked.

With no vault the card lists the vaults as blocks and the page says Pick a vault to start. over three notebooks fanned on the stone, with no pen, and offers the vaults as tiles, New Vault… and Choose Container…, the system's folder panel. With a vault and no note, the page is emaki's home page with Obsidian's new tab on it: the icon's picture (a notebook on the icon's plump pebble of onyx, a pen that writes on it while the pointer is over, on a spring as emaki's handscroll is), the greeting by the hour and the account's first name in the note's face, and under them what can be done next, each on a pill with its key at the right: New Note, New Folder, Reopen Closed Tab while there is one.
- **A rename** is a sheet over the window: a card with the name in a field, Cancel and Rename, ↩ and Esc on caps under it, on a scrim any press puts away.

## Menus

Every right click has the same card and the same grammar, after emaki's, and a new menu follows it.

- **Every choice has an icon, from what it does**, so the words stand in one column. The same act has the same icon everywhere: Rename the pencil (`pencil-simple`), a path copied the link (`link`), not the copy, since a path is an address; Copy `copy`, Cut `scissors`, Paste `clipboard`, Select All `select-all`; Copy Text `typography`, Copy Markdown `markdown`; opening a thing `external-link`, showing it in the file manager `folder-open`; Close Tab `close`, closing several `circle-x`; New `file-plus` and `folder-plus`; Collapse All Folders `copy-minus`; Move to Trash `trash`.
- **A menu of more than three is in groups, a line between**, always in this order, leaving out what does not apply: what changes the thing's name; what makes something in it; what copies it; what opens or shows it; what closes it; and what cannot be taken back, last and alone under a line of its own. A text's menu is what acts on the selection, then Select All.
- **Choices of one kind with one answer are one row** (`menu::Entry::Icons`): the kind's name, then an icon each, the one chosen lit in the accent and each named on the pointer. A piece's menu has how its link shows (Show As), its size and its place this way, first, before the groups above, so it stays short.
- **A menu says what can be done now.** Paste is there with something to paste, Cut only where the text can change, Open only for what is not showing.
- **The words are few**, as the names say: Rename, not Rename…, since the field that follows is no question.

## Motion

Every change a person asks for that moves, resizes, shows or hides something plays as a motion, never a cut, and it plays logically: the thing goes from where it is to where it will be on one eased clock, a second press while it moves turns it back from where it has got with no jump, what the two states share moves while what only one has fades, and nothing that the person did not ask for moves. A motion is there to show what changed and where it went, so a change the eye can follow (a width, a column, a plate) moves, and a change it cannot (the words rewrapping) simply follows.

The studio's patterns and timings (`widgets.rs`): a menu in over 140 ms and out over 110, a fold and a switch over 200, a segmented control's plate, the sidebar and a note's width over 220, a page's content fading and rising in over 240, every one eased with `ease_out_quint`. In Onyx: the note's sheet eases between its three widths, its gutter with it, the words wrapping as it goes; the sidebar's column opens from the rail while the sidebar fades in over the last part of the way and the rail out over the first, and the two buttons and the traffic lights the two share slide between their places; the notes card flies out beside the rail; the find bar slides in under the path bar; a folder's children fold open under its row and close back to it, the first sight playing nothing; the mode's plate slides between the three; a page (a stage's page, a note) fades and rises in when it arrives, keyed on the note; a menu comes in rising and goes out fading where it was; the rename sheet fades in over its scrim; a notice is drawn while it lasts. Left as they are on purpose: the editor's own text as it is typed, the caret, and a scroll the caret asks for, which is at once so the caret is never out of sight.

## Checking

Shoot every state a change touches, in both appearances and in a long language, and look at it whole and close up (`docs/probing.md`). Then go round again: what is out of line, louder than it needs to be, or not consistent with its neighbors is fixed and shot again, until nothing is left to change.
